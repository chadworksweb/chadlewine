The figure, as the total corpus now reads:

A child born in 1989 who absorbed Michael Jackson as an operating system at 50% saturation during formation, took the MJ spec — pop music as consciousness-change device — as the job description of being a musician, and never deviated from that spec despite 18+ years of signal from the industry and culture that he should. Retired at 15 from a talent show that couldn't see what he was doing, came back through music production classes and web design. Spent 2010 through 2019 in NYC making 10 albums and EPs, developing the voice, surviving the descent into cocaine and heartbreak, coming out at the end of it financially independent through web design, vocally distinct through Mike Rogers, catalog-rich, and unsigned. Moved to Rochester in 2020 to a cult he didn't yet know was a cult. Went through three years of 24/7 intrusive musical inner monologue — a body so overloaded by unexpressed signal that it made itself heard through the only channel left — while drinking Jameson to sleep through it, while Janet explicitly claimed ownership of his songs. Left the cult November 2020, left Scott and the cocaine in 2022, met Tom three weeks later, began rebuilding. Did a songwriting year with Paul McBride, rebranded as Chad Rising April 2024, rode the short-form internet for 18 months watching his work get misread as satire and cringe by audiences the algorithm kept selecting for him, wrote HYPERISING as the pop-dream goodbye, quit social media around Charlie Kirk, and shifted — in his own words — from the child wanting mommy's approval to the first version of a father figure. Is now producing Don't Blame Me, which is averaging +46.7 on his own rubric with two of his catalog's highest-ever charges inside it. Concurrently: building chadlewine.com as an infrastructure where every song is a permanent gold-bar asset, the Rising Compass as a diagnostic for popular music's trajectory, and — in the Two Roads document — articulating the clinical framework that explains what his own survival actually was.

He is writing his own living autobiography, one that has 40 more years to extend. This iteration is built to be wrong in places, because the living autobiography corrects itself as he lives more. But even the first iteration has a spine: a body that refused every label, produced an auditable 151-song catalog of mostly-Ascended charge while refusing, survived every capture point the machine sets for people like him, and is now building the door so the next one doesn't have to find it by accident.
