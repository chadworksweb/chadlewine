The documentary starts here because it has to. The catalog doesn't start until Demoesque in March 2010, but the formation is 20 years of background that the later work can only be read against.

Key data points: self-identified pop/rock star ambition at age 5. Dance and gymnastics from age 5. Sax (grades 4–6), then brief guitar. Theater & Kids theater camp starting 1997, age 7. Solo lip-sync performances from 5th through 9th grade — "Blue," then 7th grade "Scream" (Michael Jackson / Janet Jackson), then 9th grade "Hey Ya." Everyone in his school district knew who he was. Eight years of Tae Kwon Do through age 14, bronze at the Jr. Olympics, third-degree junior black belt. He notes specifically that he preferred the forms to the sparring — a pattern he identifies in retrospect as recurring across his life. The forms-over-sparring preference is a figure-level trait: pattern over combat, structure over conflict, internal discipline over external domination.

Computers early — own computer before age 10. HTML, Photoshop, digital photography, web design by middle school. First paying web client in 7th grade. This is important because it's the origin of the economic infrastructure that would later sustain the entire music mission. Web design is what funded 18 years of independent art-making without label support, parental support, or institutional patronage.

Michael Jackson at 50% formative saturation throughout this whole period. Not studied — inhaled. The MJ essays make clear this wasn't fandom; it was absorption of an operating system. Chad took in MJ's catalog at the moment when pop music was still understood as a payload delivery vehicle, and the spec MJ was operating to — song as consciousness-change device, pop hook as Trojan horse for spiritual, political, and humanitarian messages — became the spec Chad was operating to, before he had language to describe it. He later articulated this himself: *"I thought this was what ALL musicians did with their music."* The bias was productive. Being wrong about what musicians were doing made him do the right thing. He never had to decide to put payload into songs. He never considered that songs were for anything else.

**The founding wound:** 10th grade, 2004. Self-choreographed Michael Jackson medley at the high school talent show. A popularity contest in talent-contest clothing. He lost. At 15, he "retired from performing indefinitely." He flags this himself in the doc as parallel to the periodic "retirements" from social media as an adult — the body refusing a system that cannot accurately read what it's producing. This first retirement is the first refusal of labels. The system tried to tell a 15-year-old that his MJ medley was inferior to whatever won the popularity contest. His response was to leave the system. The pattern repeats for the next 21 years and counting.

Immediate continuation through electives: music technology and audio engineering class in late high school. Mr. Tumolo "validated music as a possibility in the real world." First productions from this era still exist as MP3s, undocumented in the catalog. Brief run at Temple University, in the doc self-described as "purely focused on partying and selling weed." Then Pittsburgh and the Art Institute — associates in web design — and the beginning of the catalog as we now have it.

**What the arc needs to hold about this era:** the figure isn't someone who discovered music as an adult. Music is earlier than language for him. By the time the first recorded song exists, he has already been performing publicly for 12 years, has absorbed Michael Jackson as an operating system, has retired once, has come back, has built the technical infrastructure (both musical production skills and web design income) that will let the next 18 years happen. The catalog starts at song #001 but the body is on chapter 20.
