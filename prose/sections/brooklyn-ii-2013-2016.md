September 2013: moves into Loft 910 in Bed Stuy, Swedish actor-director Lin Lauren managing. First immersion in a working art environment. **Daylight Animal (January 2015)** is written and mostly recorded here.

Compass reading on Daylight Animal: average +30.9. A step-change upward. Multiple ceiling-level tracks — *Cross* (+76), *Better* (+76), *Ascend* (+76), *Words of Loving* (+78) — and the emergence of what could be called spiritual-framework pop. The lyrics are unembarrassed about metaphysics. *"It's really cool that we got control of our soul in this lifetime / Not everybody knows it yet, but I'm sure that they will"* (*Ascend*). *"Weapons of destruction / Do nothing against our / Words of loving"* (*Words of Loving*). Chad identifies this era in the doc as when he was "leaning heavily into the Law of Attraction, admittedly a bit too much, which was what I now recognize as toxic positivity." The compass doesn't penalize him for that. What it registers is that the songs are doing real Ascended-tier work regardless of the over-commitment to the framework around them. The payload was accurate even when the frame was naive.

*Better* specifically at +76 is worth surfacing for the arc: it's environmental-protest pop from 2015 that reads as direct MJ-Earth-Song lineage without imitating it. "We've been pouring poison into the ocean, fish lost / I wanna see you clean up the river front / Clean up the riverfront at all costs." A 25-year-old Chad doing the MJ-heir work while the mainstream had already abandoned the tradition.

**All The Right Places (August 2015)** — 6 tracks, compass avg +30.7. The EP was written around "first back-to-back heartbreaks in NYC," including a crush going on a two-week work trip that generated *Baby Coming Back to Me* / *Back To Me*. The title track *All The Right Places* is a positive lyrical twist on "finding love in all the wrong places." Written partly in the 150-sq-ft Bushwick studio with shared kitchen and partly in the South Williamsburg luxury co-op sublet after his rent default. *Night Time Flight Time* — on SPROUT, not this EP — gets written in the lux apartment.

**SPROUT (September 2016)** — compass avg +38.8, the highest of any of the Brooklyn-era albums. Written in the Clinton Hill warehouse loft with up to 14 rotating tenants. The album takes rebirth as its explicit theme. *No Signal* is here at +78 — the song written in direct response to Sandra Bland's death. "Pulled over, no signal / Locked away and died in three days / Thrown down, choked out / You can hear him say 'I can't breathe' from his mouth." A 26-year-old white gay kid from the Philly suburbs writing Black Lives Matter-frame pop in 2015–2016, on record, before mainstream pop had caught up to the moment. One of the clearest examples in the catalog of the direct-current-event-influence category. No signal = no phone signal at the traffic stop, but also no signal from the machine, no signal that her life mattered. Multiple meanings through a single phrase. The craft is operating at a level the mainstream wasn't.

*Night Time Flight Time* at +76 is an astral-projection pop song — another one where the metaphysics isn't defended, it's assumed. *Paint What I Feel* gestures toward the visual art practice inside the catalog itself: "Painting all over, I paint what I feel." The cross-medium signal already present in the songs that reference paint.

Also during this 2015–2017 window: "sustained, first-hand exposure to the major label music industry" as assistant to an executive consultant who "was friends and colleagues with people from the VP of Republic Records to A&Rs to Sony." Inside Warner's office half a dozen times. Doc: "I wanted so badly to be a part of it, but I wasn't ever what they were looking for and later discovered it was meant to be." This is the most direct encounter with the machine in the whole narrative. Got to see the inside. Was not selected. Later understood that non-selection was protection.
