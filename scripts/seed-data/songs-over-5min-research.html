The studies below point at the same thing: moving a listener into a genuine state (absorption, flow, trance, deep emotional immersion) is a function of time. The shift is gradual and depends on staying inside the music for minutes, not seconds. A song that ends at two or three minutes can stop before the state has formed.

The research

  • Time is the variable. Jörg Fachner's work, "Time is the Key: Music and Altered States of Consciousness", frames time as the decisive factor in how music changes consciousness. The state builds as the listener stays with the music, and time perception itself bends. Therapeutic and ritual music inductions are commonly given on the order of 13 to 15 minutes to move someone into an altered state.
  • Sustained listening produces "trancing." Judith Becker's Deep Listeners (2004) describes how staying with music you are not resisting produces a transient state of being "out of time," dissociated from ordinary awareness. It is something you settle into over a span, not a switch that flips on contact.
  • Entrainment strengthens with repetition. Brainwaves synchronize to steady rhythm in the theta range (about 4 to 8 Hz), and the strength of the altered state tracks the number of repetitions rather than the content. Short clips never reach the repetition count. See neural entrainment to electronic music and altered states (Frontiers, 2025) and prolonged exposure to highly rhythmic music and brain dynamics (Neuropsychologia, 2019).
  • Emotional engagement builds across a track. In EEG studies of music and emotion, neural and emotional response keeps rising the longer a listener stays in the piece, which is why researchers use multi-minute excerpts rather than short snippets to capture a real response.
